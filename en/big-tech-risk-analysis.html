Big-Tech Risk Analysis

Are your critical business processes too dependent on big tech?

Maybe it's not a problem tomorrow. But it's smart to know today.

The core of the problem

Organizations, especially in the public sector, have become massively dependent on a small number of American cloud services: Microsoft Azure, AWS, Google Cloud. That dependency was rarely a conscious choice. It crept in, one assignment at a time.

Geopolitical risk

Tensions between countries can restrict access to services, with direct consequences for critical processes.

Outages with no fallback

When a platform goes down, operations grind to a halt. Think of the global CrowdStrike outage of June 2024.

Data outside European jurisdiction

Data sits with vendors governed by foreign law, creating tension with European regulations.

No plan B

Many organizations have become "analog incapable": there's no working fallback if the technology fails.

The Dutch situation

Researcher Bert Hubert has mapped which sectors are fully dependent on American cloud services: municipalities, the Dutch vehicle authority (RDW), the employee insurance agency (UWV) and safety regions in government; hospitals and GP out-of-hours services in healthcare (including through Chipsoft HiX); newspapers, broadcasters, banks and payment systems in media and finance; and public transport and rail, which are actively migrating to Azure and AWS.

The conclusion of that research is clear: there is no serious plan B. Large parts of the Netherlands' digital infrastructure are built on American foundations.

What I do about it

1. Risk map

Which processes grind to a halt if access to a vendor is cut off? I map it out, concretely and without alarmism.

2. Alternatives

Are there European or open-source alternatives that fit the scale and context of your organization? I research them and weigh them honestly.

3. Strategy

How do you build a less vulnerable position step by step, without turning everything upside down at once? Realistic and manageable.
